# IP and Licensing Notes — Not Legal Advice

- Copyright may protect original expression fixed in a tangible form (documentation, artwork, source code), but not ideas, systems, methods, or concepts as such.
- Patents may cover qualifying inventions meeting legal requirements such as novelty and non-obviousness. Public disclosure can affect patent options and timing.
- Trademarks identify the source of goods or services; they do not create ownership of a word in every context.
- Licenses state what others may do with covered material. Attribution/provenance document origin but are not a substitute for a license.

A practical option to discuss with counsel: Apache-2.0 for software/schemas/reference implementations (includes an express patent license); CC BY 4.0 for explanatory documentation; separate trademark policy for names/logos. Do not add license files until ownership, contributions, dependencies, and intended terms are reviewed.

Defensive publication can create prior art against later patent claims on disclosed subject matter, but publishing before filing can severely limit your own patent options. Do not disclose potentially patentable inventions solely to "claim them first" without qualified patent advice.

Practical steps: keep attributable history; require contributor provenance; use signed releases where practical; preserve third-party notices; consider copyright registration for important works; conduct trademark clearance; consult an IP attorney before patent-sensitive disclosure or portfolio-wide licensing.
